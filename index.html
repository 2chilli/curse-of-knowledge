Have you
ever talked
to someone
so unbelievably
stupid

that it irritated you?

That irritation is not about them at all.

It's a glitch in your own brain.

Psychologists call it

the Curse of knowledge AKA جحيم الوعي 🙏🥀

Once you learn something, your brain makes it extremely difficult to remember what it felt like to not know it.

Think about something you understand perfectly now.

There was a time when you were completely clueless about it.

You didn't understand the words.

You didn't see the pattern.

But after enough exposure,

something changed.

It clicked,

and now it feels obvious.

So when someone else doesn't understand it, your brain doesn't naturally experience their confusion.

Instead, it looks at the answer sitting right in front of them and thinks,

“How are you not seeing this?”

And that's where the irritation begins.

Because without realising it, you've taken your current level of knowledge and projected it backward onto everyone else.

← projected backward

Everyone else

You forget that you once didn't know either.

The smarter you become at something,

the harder it can be to understand what it's like for someone who's still learning it.

So think about that before you let someone's stupidity anger you.

Or don't.

Accuracy notes

  1. "Psychologists call it the curse of knowledge." The term is usually credited to economists Colin Camerer, George Loewenstein and Martin Weber (1989). Psychology research now uses it widely. The slide keeps the video's wording.
  2. "A glitch in your own brain" is the video's metaphor. The curse of knowledge is a cognitive bias, not a malfunction.
  3. The post caption, "you aren't insane for being smart", is not said in the video, so it is not on the slides.
  4. The video has no statistics. The chart on slide 10 is a concept sketch, not data.
